      How it works
      Everything a SketchUp file needs before a slicer can read it, in one pass.
      
    
      1
      Upload the .skp
      Or export STL/OBJ yourself. The SketchUp file is opened by the official SketchUp SDK in an isolated, low-privilege process; hidden tags and hidden objects are skipped.
      2
      Diagnose at your print scale
      Open faces, edges shared by three or more faces, flipped faces, overlapping groups, loose pieces, and parts that become thinner than a nozzle at 1:N.
      3
      Rebuild one closed solid
      The model is rebuilt as a single watertight solid that keeps the outer surface. Free-standing parts too thin to print are removed; relief on walls is kept.
      4
      Verify and download
      The result must have 0 open and 0 non-manifold edges. Its deviation from your model is measured; you get an STL to slice.
      
  
      

  
      
    
      Why SketchUp models fail to print
      SketchUp is a surface modeller: what looks solid on screen is often a set of separate faces. These are the usual reasons a slicer rejects a building.
      
    
      Single faces as walls
      A wall drawn as one face has no thickness. Slicers see a hole, not a wall.
      Fix Walls are rebuilt with material on the inside of the facade.
      Groups that only touch
      Each group or component is its own shell. Where they meet, edges are shared by 3+ faces: non-manifold.
      Fix Touching and overlapping groups are merged into one body.
      Overlapping and intersecting geometry
      Floors pushed through walls, roofs through gables. Double walls and inner faces confuse slicers.
      Fix Inner faces disappear; only the outer skin is kept.
      Too thin at the chosen scale
      A 50 mm railing is 0.25 mm at 1:200. It cannot print, but it keeps the model from being one solid.
      Fix Free-standing thin parts are removed and listed; nothing on a wall surface is touched.
      Reversed faces
      The grey back side facing out. Some slicers fill the wrong side.
      Fix The rebuilt surface is oriented outward everywhere.
      Wrong units or stray geometry
      Models drawn in the wrong unit, tiny leftover edges, 2D people and trees.
      Fix Scale presets, size preview at print scale, specks smaller than a nozzle dropped.

      FAQ
      
    
      Which SketchUp versions are supported?
      Files saved by SketchUp 2017 or newer open directly. If a file is rejected, save it again from a recent SketchUp, or export STL (File > Export > 3D Model).
      What happens to hidden tags, layers and objects?
      Hidden objects and objects on hidden tags are skipped, exactly as in the SketchUp view. Hide people, trees and furniture before uploading if you do not want them printed.
      Which scale should I choose?
      Pick the 1:N you will print at. The model is shrunk first, then checked, so anything that is too thin at that scale is found. The tool shows the print size before you upload.
      Is the .skp file safe on your side?
      SketchUp files are opened on our processing PC in an isolated low-integrity process with no access to other files, a memory cap and a time limit. All files are deleted after 24 hours.
      Does the shape change?
      The surface is rebuilt at about 0.2 mm, so detail finer than that softens. The median and 95th-percentile deviation from your model are reported for every job.
      What does the request box do?
      With a sentence, an agent plans the repair, for example removing railings but keeping lettering. Agent jobs have a daily limit; without a request the automatic pipeline runs.
      Is it free? Can I use the results commercially?
      Yes. The beta is free and supported by ads, and you keep all rights to your models and to the results.
      Is the source code available?
      Not yet. The processing code and our own example models will be published later; for now the service is used through this page.
